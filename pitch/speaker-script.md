## Script

**1. RepairFair** (Safaa)

We're RepairFair. Workshops send quotes by text, and drivers approve them line by line.

**2. The job** (Safaa)

Meet Mia. Her brakes need doing, so she drops her car at a local workshop. The estimate is three hundred dollars. She says yes. [Next card.] Mid-morning, her phone rings. It's the workshop, and they're doing the right thing by calling. The rotors are bad too. And the caliper needs a service. [Next cards.] Mia's at work, and the explanation is rushed. She's confused. But she needs her car for the commute, so she says yes. She isn't sure what she's just approved.

**3. The bill** (Safaa)

When she picks it up, the bill is eleven hundred and twenty. [Pause.] What did she actually approve? Mia's made up. The numbers aren't: a Sydney driver really was quoted three hundred and billed eleven hundred and twenty for brakes.

**4. 1 in 5** (Safaa)

Mia isn't alone. We read over a thousand bad reviews of Sydney mechanics. One in five of them is about this: a surprise bill, work nobody approved, or work nobody needed. And those reviews sit on the workshop's own Google page.

**5. The insight** (Safaa)

But most people trust their local mechanic. So trust isn't the problem. The missing record is. The mechanic stops work and gets a rushed yes. The driver can't check it, and the workshop can't prove it. One customer denied approving a sixty-dollar brake fluid change, and the workshop had to absorb it.

**6. The solution** (Safaa)

So we built RepairFair. Every line, approved in writing. Here's one job, start to finish.

**7. Demo** (Video)

Plays with its own voice. Stay silent.

**8. Same job** (Safaa)

Same brakes. This time it's on her phone, so she can read it properly. The pads are urgent and the rotors can wait. Every line on the bill is one she approved, and the mechanic has proof.

**9. Why now** (Safaa)

This works now because AI can turn a mechanic's rough notes into a clear quote in seconds. The mechanic checks it, and it goes out. The notes they already write become the quote, and the driver's yes is saved as the record.

**10. Traction** (Safaa)

We took it to ten workshops. Six want to try it. Four said they'd pay. Workshops told us it would save time and paperwork, cut disputes, and build trust with customers. That's who we're for: honest workshops. We're talking to two about a free trial, and they'd decide whether to pay afterwards. We haven't got paying customers yet, and that's the next step. [Hand over to Sameep.]

**11. Competition** (Sameep)

We're for small workshops that still run approvals on paper, phone calls, email and messages. We replace their paper quote, the phone call and the paper invoice. Others offer approval, but only inside a full system. We make sure the driver understands, and the workshop can prove it.

**12. Business model** (Sameep)

Workshops pay forty-nine dollars a month. Drivers pay nothing, and small workshops start free. We believe a customer who understands says yes faster. You get paid for the extras, make up to fifty fewer calls a month by our estimate, and a "not now" comes back as a reminder. One hour of that work covers the fee.

**13. Market** (Sameep)

We start with six pilot workshops, then thirty from the ninety-six we've found in the Inner West. Sydney comes once the pilot hits its target. Then a light system, a trusted network of garages, and a membership tier with parts supply, including hard-to-find parts sourced direct from overseas. With that network behind us, we move past independents and take on the big players head-on.

**14. Team** (Sameep)

We're Safaa and Sameep, for Group 8. We've interviewed ten workshops and built a prototype. Sameep has also sourced car parts from China.

**15. Ask** (Sameep)

Now we need real workshops. Help us run one pilot: six Inner West garages, three months. It's a success if three of them put a card on file. Because the next time someone like Mia walks up to that counter, the bill shouldn't be a surprise.

## Likely questions
- **How was the review number checked?** It was AI-coded, and two more AI coders agreed on 90%+ of a 60-review sample. Say a person hasn't checked it, unless the human check gets done.
- **Is Mia real?** Mia is a made-up character, and so is her phone call, including the rotors and caliper. The $300 quote and $1,120 bill come from a real Sydney driver's brake job in our research, and the $470 bill is the demo.
- **Was the $1,120 a rip-off?** We don't know, and that's the point. A typical brake job is about $450–600 on AutoGuru, but extra work can be genuine. Without a written record, the driver can't tell.
- **Do people trust their mechanic?** Yes, 71% do in AAAA's research. That's our point: the problem is the missing record, not dishonest mechanics.
- **Isn't this already required?** NSW advice is to get a quote in writing, and repairers must only do authorised work. We make that record easy to create and keep.
- **Isn't another startup doing this?** Yes, there are recent startups offering full workshop systems. That's a good sign the problem is real. They replace your system, and we add one step.
- **Aren't you more expensive than Workshop Software?** Our $49 is below its plans, but for a workshop already on one, it's an extra cost. Don't say "we're cheaper". If you're on a top-tier system, we're probably not for you yet. Our first customers are the workshops on paper or basic software. For them it's about what Workshop Software's two-way texting add-on costs alone, with nothing to switch.
- **How many workshops have no software?** Of the five workshops where we recorded it, three had no workshop software. That's a small sample, so we wouldn't scale it up, but they're our first customers.
- **Why would dodgy workshops use this?** They wouldn't, and they're not our customer. We're for honest workshops, who lose money when a customer says "I never agreed to that". Two of our ten had lost money on extras.
- **Isn't 1 in 5 a small share of all reviews?** Yes. Bad reviews are about 7% of all reviews, so it's about 1.4% overall. It shows what people complain about, not how often it happens. For how often, five of ten bills in our driver interviews rose by more than 10%, which is a small sample.
- **Do drivers choose garages because of this?** We have no evidence they do. Only 1 of 25 drivers got a second quote, so we sell to workshops, not to drivers shopping around.
- **Doesn't Workshop Software already have an approval link?** Yes, on Gold and above, as part of its inspections. Its help page says customers can approve or reject each item, and Workshop Hub's Shop plan does the same. So line-by-line approval isn't our difference. Not needing a full system is. We haven't tried either product.
- **Who else does this?** Several Australian systems offer customer approval, including Workshop Software, Workshop Hub and OneBookPlus. Workshop Hub also has AI quotes and voice notes. All of them need you to use their system. We start with small workshops that run approvals on paper, phone calls, email and messages.
- **How are you different from Workshop Hub?** It's a full system for running a workshop, and it's live. We're a prototype built around the moment a driver says yes: urgency and a plain reason on each line, their own language, and no full system to learn. We haven't tried its product, so we talk about what we do.
- **Why pay for a system and for you?** We start with workshops that run approvals on paper, phone calls, email and messages. For those without software, we're their first tool, not a second bill. Two workshops that already use software also said they'd consider paying, at up to about $50 a month.
- **Doesn't workshop software already solve this?** Not always. One workshop we spoke to had MechanicDesk and still got approvals by phone and messages. That's the workshop that absorbed the sixty dollars.
- **Are you an add-on or a system?** Today we replace the paper quote, the phone call and the paper invoice. Next comes a light system, once the pilot works. An add-on for workshops with software stays an option, but it isn't our first market.
- **Does transparency really win customers?** We believe a clear written quote helps a new customer trust a workshop they don't know yet. We have no proof, so the pilot would compare how often new and regular customers approve work.
- **What will you do with the data?** With consent from workshops and drivers, approval records could show which garages quote clearly and how prices compare. Workshops couldn't pay for a better ranking, or the trust goes. It's a plan for later, not something we've built.
- **How do drivers benefit?** They stay free. Later we plan a car history across garages, a way to find trusted garages, a fair-price check from real jobs, and verified reviews. Only 1 of 25 drivers got a second quote, so drivers may not shop around. That's why it's a later stage.
- **Why not just use a cheap full system like OneBookPlus?** Many workshops should, if they're happy to switch. OneBookPlus's approval add-on can cost less than we do. Our customers are workshops that won't change what they use, such as paper, or accounting software like Xero or MYOB. We haven't proven that a simpler start beats that, and we don't know how many of these workshops exist. The pilot will test it.
- **Why would a workshop pay if the problem is the driver's?** Because a confused customer costs the workshop money. A customer who understands the quote can say yes in writing, and we believe they'll do it faster. The workshop gets paid for the extras, because it can prove the yes, and two of ten had lost money on them. It spends less time chasing, and three of ten struggle to reach customers. A "not now" becomes a reminder, not a lost job. One hour of that work covers the fee, by our estimate.
- **Won't customers just reject more work?** Maybe, and we don't know yet. Declined items become reminders, and the pilot will show what happens.
- **Do pilot garages pay?** Not during the pilot. Our target is three of six putting a card on file by the end, with billing starting after their free months. That's a target, not a result.
- **What will the pilot measure?** Time spent explaining quotes, whether final bills match what was approved, and whether drivers understood what they approved. A driver declining an item isn't success, and a higher bill can be fine if it was authorised. It also tests whether workshops save enough time to justify paying.
- **You promised a 10-day pilot with two workshops. What happened?** [Sameep: add the real reason.] We don't have pilot results. Say that plainly, then give any written commitments we have.
- **Your Week 11 feedback form says 6 interviews, 4 trials and 2 willing to pay. Why 10, 6 and 4?** Our interview sheet and Week 11 script both say ten workshops, six would trial and four would pay. We can show the sheet with workshop names removed.
- **How are you different from workshop software?** We help the driver decide, with urgency, a plain reason and a typical price on each line. Those prices are sample data for now. There's no full system to learn, and there's a free plan. Drivers can also view the quote in their own language in the prototype, though we haven't tested that with customers yet. Next we plan extras-only use and an approval receipt for both sides. We haven't tried competitors' products, so we talk about what we do, not what they lack.
- **What if the AI gets a quote wrong?** The mechanic checks every quote before it goes out, and the workshop stays responsible for it. AI writes it up, and the mechanic decides.
- **What would stop a competitor copying you?** Nothing technical. Approvals help sell their higher plans, so they have little reason to offer them cheaply to workshops that won't buy a full system. That's our reasoning, not proof. Our evidence is one MechanicDesk workshop that still ran approvals by phone and messages. Speed in one area is our only head start, so we don't claim a moat yet. The bigger risk is newer, cheaper systems that already bundle approval.
- **What about EVs?** An EV is just another car to us. It's a vehicle type in the same product, not a second market. The app has no EV option yet, so it's planned.
- **How do you reach $820k?** It takes about 1,400 paying workshops, so it depends on going national. It's an illustration, not a forecast.
- **How big can this get?** At $49, every independent in Australia is worth about $16.5M a year, or about $26.5M at $79. Past independents come chains and dealers, which we haven't sized yet. The $10B is what drivers and fleets spend on repairs, not what we'd earn.
- **Is 28,000 a conservative number?** No. It's AAAA's 27,700 independents, and scaling up NSW licences gives a rough 22,000. At 22,000, $49 is about $12.9M a year. The conservative numbers are the 96 and 607 we found ourselves.
- **Are there really 28,000 workshops?** About. AAAA says 27,700 independents and IBISWorld says about 29,000 businesses, but that includes sole traders and specialists, so our real market is smaller.
- **Is the price too low?** It might be. Later, a higher membership tier would add parts supply. We'd also raise prices for new workshops as the network grows, while founding workshops keep $49. Four workshops said $29–79, so we moved from the $29–49 we showed in Week 11 to $49. We'll log support and training hours for each workshop in the pilot, and test $79 on later workshops.
- **What does a quote cost you?** About 20c, almost all of it two text messages. The AI is under half a cent. Hosting is about $20–105 a month. These are estimates from price lists, because we haven't deployed yet.
- **Where does fifty fewer calls come from?** It's an estimate, not a measurement. We assume 100 quotes a month, and about half need a mid-job approval, based on 5 of 10 bills rising more than 10% in our driver interviews. "Up to" matters, because some customers will still want to talk. If each call takes about five minutes, that's roughly four hours, before counting the minute or so each app approval takes. The pilot would measure the real time.
- **What are your margins?** At 100 quotes a month, we keep about $25 of the $49, roughly half, before card fees and support. The 100 quotes is an assumption.
- **What stops one busy workshop costing you more than $49?** Pro includes 300 texts a month, about 150 quotes, then 12c a text. Without a limit we'd lose money from about 245 quotes.
- **When do you make money?** Founding workshops start paying after six months, and the free plan stops at 20 quotes. The offer costs about $4,300 for 30 workshops, which is illustrative.
- **Why not take a cut of each job?** We'd earn more when drivers say yes. A flat fee keeps us neutral. Parts supply would come later as a higher membership tier, so it's still a flat fee, not a cut of the jobs drivers approve.
- **Is 607 all of Sydney?** No, it's the workshops we found across 21 councils, so it's a minimum. The real number is much higher.
- **Do fleets or insurers want this?** Not now. We haven't spoken to any, and the pilot stays on workshops as the payers. If we get there, fleets come first, because the business paying isn't the driver, so it has Mia's problem. Insurers are mostly crash repairs, so they're a possible later option.
- **Is the Xero connection real?** Not yet. The demo shows the planned flow.
- **Is the invoice a real tax invoice?** Not yet. It's a prototype invoice with a sample ABN, and it hasn't been checked against tax invoice rules. It's built only from approved lines, and marking it paid moves no money.
- **Do workshops have to enter jobs twice?** Paper workshops don't: in the prototype, the approved quote becomes the invoice. The Xero export file hasn't been tested in Xero, and we don't connect to MYOB. Workshops with full software would enter jobs twice, because we haven't built a direct connection.
- **Have any workshops signed up?** We're talking to two about a free trial, and they'd decide whether to pay afterwards. Neither has committed in writing yet.
- **Would paper workshops really pay?** We don't know for sure. Four workshops said they'd pay, and two of those already use workshop software. In our notes, the pen-and-paper workshop said no and two on templates didn't commit. The pilot needs to test this.
- **Isn't selling parts a conflict of interest?** It's a membership tier, not a cut of each job. Workshops pay a higher flat monthly fee for access to our parts suppliers, so we earn nothing more when a driver says yes. Drivers still see every line and approve it.
- **Aren't cheap imported parts risky, especially brakes?** Yes, so quality comes first. We'd start with rare and hard-to-find parts from vetted suppliers, and only list parts that meet Australian standards, with a clear warranty.
- **Can you really compete with the big players?** Not today. We start where they don't fit: small workshops on paper, calls and messages. Once our garage network and parts tier are big enough, we compete head-on. Sameep's experience sourcing parts is part of that edge.
