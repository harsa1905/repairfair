The story takes about 4:13 with pauses. Presenter 1 tells Mia's story through to the new bill. Presenter 2 covers why it can work as a business. Leave the slides to carry the numbers, and pause after the $1,120.

## Script

**1. RepairFair** (Presenter 1)

We're RepairFair. Workshops send quotes by text, and drivers approve them line by line.

**2. The quote** (Presenter 1)

Meet Mia. Her brakes need doing, so she drops her car at a local workshop. The quote is three hundred dollars. She says yes.

**3. The bill** (Presenter 1)

When she comes back, the bill is eleven hundred and twenty. [Pause.] Mia's made up. That bill isn't. A Sydney driver really got it.

**4. The trap** (Presenter 1)

And that's the trap. The work is already done. She can't tell what she's paying for, and she can't send it back.

**5. No answer** (Presenter 1)

Now stand on the other side of the counter. The mechanic isn't the villain here. They find a problem and ring Mia. She's at work. No answer. The car sits on the hoist, the next job waits, and if they go ahead anyway, she can say she never agreed.

**6. 1 in 5** (Presenter 1)

We read over a thousand bad reviews of Sydney mechanics. One in five is about this: a surprise bill, work nobody approved, or work nobody needed.

**7. The insight** (Presenter 1)

But most people trust their local mechanic. So trust isn't the problem. The missing record is. Repairs get agreed in a quick phone call, and nobody can check what was said.

**8. The solution** (Presenter 1)

So we built RepairFair. Every line, approved in writing. Here's one job, start to finish.

**9. Demo** (Video)

Plays with its own voice. Stay silent.

**10. Mia's new bill** (Presenter 1)

Same brakes. Same Mia. This time, every line on the bill is one she approved, and the mechanic has proof.

**11. Why now** (Presenter 2)

This works now because AI can read a mechanic's rough notes and write a clear quote in seconds. The mechanic checks it, and it goes out.

**12. Traction** (Presenter 2)

We took it to ten workshops. Six want to try it. Four said they'd pay. They also changed what we built: written approval came from owners who'd lost money on extras. We've got a working prototype. We haven't got paying customers yet, and that's the next step.

**13. Competition** (Presenter 2)

Workshop software does far more than we do, and owners rarely switch. So we don't ask them to. Keep your system, and add the part that gets the yes. We help the driver decide what's urgent, what can wait, and why.

**14. Business model** (Presenter 2)

Workshops pay forty-nine dollars a month. Drivers pay nothing. When a driver says "not now", we remind them later. About one hour of that work a month covers the fee.

**15. Market** (Presenter 2)

Australians spend about ten billion dollars a year on servicing and repairs, across twenty-eight thousand workshops. We start in Sydney's Inner West, where garages sit close together, and go suburb by suburb. Every garage that joins makes a clear quote something drivers look for.

**16. Team** (Presenter 2)

We're [names]. This semester we've interviewed ten workshops, read over a thousand reviews, mapped six hundred garages and built a working prototype.

**17. Ask** (Presenter 2)

Now we need real workshops. Help us run one pilot: six Inner West garages, three months. Because the next time someone like Mia walks up to that counter, the bill shouldn't be a surprise.

## Likely questions
- **How was the review number checked?** It was AI-coded, and two more AI coders agreed on 90%+ of a 60-review sample. Say a person hasn't checked it, unless the human check gets done.
- **Is Mia real?** Mia is a made-up character. The $300 quote and $1,120 bill come from a real Sydney driver in our research, and the $470 bill is the demo.
- **Do people trust their mechanic?** Yes, 71% do in AAAA's research. That's our point: the problem is the missing record, not dishonest mechanics.
- **Isn't this already required?** NSW advice is to get a quote in writing, and repairers must only do authorised work. We make that record easy to create and keep.
- **Isn't another startup doing this?** Yes, there are recent startups offering full workshop systems. That's a good sign the problem is real. They replace your system, and we add one step.
- **What would stop a competitor copying you?** Features can be copied. Our focus is workshops that won't switch, and a full system has little reason to plug into rivals.
- **How do you reach $820k?** It takes about 1,400 paying workshops, so it depends on going national. It's an illustration, not a forecast.
- **How big can this get?** At $49, Australian workshops alone are worth about $16.5M a year. The larger pool is the $10 billion spent on repairs, with fleets and insurers as later options.
- **Is the price too low?** We don't know yet, which is one thing the pilot would test, along with support costs.
- **Is the Xero connection real?** Not yet. The demo shows the planned flow.
