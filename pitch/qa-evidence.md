# Q&A and app evidence (collected 31 Oct 2026)

Facts for judges' questions and for replacing the app's sample numbers. Everything here is from public pages read on 31 Oct 2026. The quality column matters: vendor blogs are weak evidence, so say so if asked.

## Competitors

**Meckly changes our competition story.** It's an Australian workshop system selling almost the same promise. Its page for car owners opens on a surprise-bill story and offers quotes by SMS with photos, approved before work starts.

| Product | Price a month (ex GST) | Approval by the customer | Driver record | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Meckly** | $99, $199 or $299 by shop size (launch $89, $169, $259). 30 days free. 150–500 free texts | SMS quote approval with photos, free for drivers | Free invoices and history; a logbook across shops for $7 a car a month or $60 a year | Its own pages [1][2] |
| **Workshop Software** | Silver $119 ($99 yearly), Gold $149 ($119 yearly). 14-day trial | Gold and above: inspection link the customer opens to approve. Two-way SMS add-on from $49 | Not found | Its own pages [3][4] |
| **MechanicDesk** | From $85 | A competitor says it couldn't find a customer approval link, and that SMS is one-way | Not found | Competitor's claim [4][5] |
| **Auxo Workshop** | NZ$99 | Not checked | Not found | Own page [6] |
| **AutoGuru** | Marketplace, fees not found | Drivers compare quotes and book | Not found | Startup Daily, undated [7] |

**Still unknown:** whether any of them does AI drafting from rough notes, item-by-item approval with urgency levels, or a typical price check. "Not found" doesn't mean they lack it.

**What's left of our edge:**
1. **Nothing to switch.** Meckly and the others are full workshop systems that replace what a shop uses. We're a $49 add-on, and workshops rarely switch. This is the strongest point.
2. **Rough notes to an itemised quote,** checked by the mechanic.
3. **Item-by-item choice,** with urgency and reasons, on a plain text link.

**What no longer works:** "the driver side they don't have", and the driver-owned record as our main defence, because Meckly already sells one. The independent network still has value, but Meckly is ahead on it.

**Useful side of this:** a company is already charging for this, which helps show the problem is real.

## Trust: a likely challenge

**71% of consumers say they trust their local mechanic** (AAAA [8]). A judge could use this against us. It comes from an industry body, and our point isn't that mechanics are dishonest. Our answer: trust in a local mechanic and a clear written record aren't opposites, and our 12 survey responses show drivers still don't check quotes.

## The law

| Question | Answer | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Isn't this already required?" | NSW advice is to get a quote in writing so there are no surprises, and says a repairer must only do authorised work. So the rule exists, but nothing makes the record easy to create or keep | NSW Government [10] |
| Who handles disputes? | NSW Fair Trading offers free dispute resolution, and publishes a complaints register | NSW Government [11] |
| Can we text reminders? | Marketing texts need consent and a working unsubscribe, actioned within 5 working days, with no login required. The business stays liable even if a provider sends them. Follow-up reminders are probably commercial messages, so consent must be built in | ACMA [12] |

**Not found:** an official count of car repair complaints. The complaints register may allow one, but it would need a manual search.

## Workshop margins

There's no ATO benchmark for general mechanics. The closest is automotive electrical services (2023–24) [13]:

| Share of turnover, by business size | Under $250k | $250–750k | Over $750k |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost of sales, average | 35% | 38% | 41% |
| Labour, typical range | 19–29% | 16–25% | 20–28% |
| Total expenses, average | 66% | 73% | 83% |

So on a $100 job, about **$60 is left after parts**, and about **$35–45 after parts and wages**. This replaces the US 50% figure. At 40%, $49 needs about **$120 of work**, which is about an hour at metro labour rates. The new line: **"One hour of follow-up work a month covers the $49."** It's still a proxy, so one real workshop's answer would be better.

| Bendix front or rear brake service | From $249 | Whole job, national chain | mycar [18] | |
| Pads, bargain, mid-grade, performance | $40–60, $90–130, $150–220 an axle | Parts, one source | [15] | $70–120, $130–190, $180–260 |
| Rotors, standard sedan | $50–80 a side | Parts, one source | [15] | $200–320 a pair |
| Brake job time | 60–90 minutes | Time | [15] | 1.5 hours |

**Recommendation for the app:**
- **Labour:** change the range to $120–180. $90 is below every metro figure, and a mechanic in the room would notice.
- **Demo job:** the app session suggested $140 an hour, making $370. That goes above the published $150–350 for pads only. **$120 an hour for 1.5 hours gives $340**, which fits every source.
- **Parts:** only one source, so keep the sample ranges and their "sample" label. Rotors look high against that source.
- **Not found:** brake fluid flush, brake hose and wheel alignment prices.

## Results screen benchmarks

These are invented in the app and stay labelled as samples. No reliable public benchmarks were found for approval rates or win-back of declined work. Vendors only say approvals rise, without solid numbers. **Don't show the sample Results figures as evidence.**

Meckly claims most customers respond within the hour [19]. That supports the waiting problem, but it's a vendor's claim.

## Costs (rough)

Text messages cost workshops about 10–11c through competitors [1][3][5]. Our own supplier rate and AI cost per quote haven't been checked.
